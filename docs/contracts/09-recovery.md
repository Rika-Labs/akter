# Failure and recovery

**Responsibility:** define behavior across crashes and restarts.  
**Authority:** normative.  
**Owner role:** reliability.  
**Change policy:** every new durable record needs a crash-point analysis.

Cluster runner registration, shard ownership, and table-backed singleton lease checks MUST use the same deployment coordination authority, even when actor data lives on different databases. Releasing a resource or rolling back an interrupted coordination transaction MUST admit its waiting contender without requiring deletion of its lock identity. A detected coordination-connection failure MUST abort guarded work that has not committed; same-data-database replacements MUST still wait for the old data transaction's local fence. This is not atomic commit between coordination and data databases, and a lost acknowledgment MUST be resolved by rereading durable data ([ADR 0066](../decisions/0066-authoritative-coordination.md)).

Recovery MUST derive authority and consequences from durable rows, never process memory. Before-COMMIT failure MUST roll back the receipt, state, events, intents, and jobs. An intent from such a turn MUST never be delivered. After-COMMIT reply loss MUST resolve through the receipt without re-running the handler; external result delivery remains subject to the access and expiry rules in [receipts](04-receipts.md). A crash after a job executor succeeds but before its `onSuccess` route commits MUST leave the job pending: if the result was not yet recorded, the executor runs again under the same job id; if it was, the route is redelivered and its receipt deduplicates it ([background work](08-background-work.md)). Built by M4.14 ([ADR 0035](../decisions/0035-pglite-embedded-production-backend.md), accepted): on file-backed PGlite a SIGKILL at any point MUST recover to the last committed transaction on the next start, and a second process on the same `dataDir` MUST be refused before it opens the database.

A retryable turn failure MUST restart the activation and then answer the attempt with the retryable `ActorUnavailable`; an uncommitted command is not durable, and the caller's handle MUST retry it with the same command id. The answer MUST NOT depend on Cluster re-sending the command to a restarted entity, which can drop a re-sent command whose turn fails again before the restart completes. Committed intents MUST be recovered from `actor_outbox`. Deterministic defects MUST follow [command turn](02-command-turns.md) rules and leave the activation resident. Hibernation MUST discard activation-local values while preserving committed state, parked connections, and connection sessions. A graceful move of the actor's owner MUST keep connections held by other runners open; they resume on the new owner with the last committed session, and frames are redelivered in order, changing the stored session at most once. Every activation end other than process death, including defect restarts and eviction, MUST flush and seal its broadcasts. An ungraceful owner death MUST keep connections open and resync them in place with a `Resync` frame carrying the last proven event cursor, closing with `SessionEnded` cause `OwnerLost` only when the client does not acknowledge within 30 seconds or the resync cannot run. When a holder dies, its connections close without running `close` handlers, and their `actor_connections` rows MUST be deleted within one sweep after the holder's registration expires, including when it restarts at the same address ([ADR 0023](../decisions/0023-connections-parking-and-streams.md)).

After ungraceful runner loss in the expiry-based recovery path, a survivor MUST wait for the dead runner's lock to expire before acquiring ownership; stale generations MUST fail the database fence. A singleton's residency and background loop MUST move to one surviving runner. Its cron ticks are outbox rows that any surviving relay claims, and they are delivered once the singleton is resident again ([ADR 0021](../decisions/0021-multi-runner-relay-singleton-and-cron.md)). Tests MUST distinguish lock expiry from time until resumed service, including polling and activation. The `shardLockExpiration` recovery target remains gated, not a measured end-to-end availability guarantee.

Outbox recovery MUST resume undelivered `actor_outbox` rows using the original intent id, on any runner, once the dead relay's claim lease has ended. A job attempt whose runner died keeps its unknown outcome (`ambiguous`) and is retried or dead-lettered by another runner after its lease; if it was cancelled, it is settled as `Unknown` and `ambiguous` without running again, and its per-actor cap slot frees when the lease ends ([ADR 0024](../decisions/0024-effect-cancellation-and-per-actor-concurrency.md)). A crash after the receiver commits but before the sender's row is deleted MUST NOT repeat the receiver's transition; its receipt replays.

Restore from a backup is recovery from durable rows too: the restored snapshot is the only authority, so every runner MUST be stopped before it is restored, and an actor's first turn afterwards MUST take a generation above the snapshot's. Receipts, pending intents, timers, and jobs in the snapshot MUST be recovered as after a crash, a job in flight at the backup keeping its job id and attempt count, and an expired external id MUST stay refused whether or not the snapshot holds its receipt. Turns committed after the snapshot are lost to the database; their external consequences are reconciled by the operator ([backup and restore](../operations/04-backup-restore.md)).

Postgres shutdown, crash recovery, startup refusal (`57P01`–`57P03`), and temporary resource exhaustion (`53000`–`53300`) MUST follow the retryable-turn path even when the driver classifies them as unknown SQL errors. A dropped database (`57P04`) or a configuration limit (`53400`) MUST NOT become retryable merely because it shares an SQLSTATE prefix. Ordinary driver-classified retryable errors remain retryable.

`Database.postgres` requests server TCP keepalives on turn, off-turn, and replica sessions so a vanished runner does not leave session advisory locks held until the operating system's long idle timeout. The defaults are 5 seconds idle, 2 seconds between probes, and 3 failed probes; caller settings take precedence. These settings are not an end-to-end recovery deadline, and apply to TCP sessions, not Unix sockets. The restart and partition drills and their limitations are recorded in [BENCHMARKS.md](../../BENCHMARKS.md).

Required evidence is listed in [failure cases](../verification/02-failure-matrix.md) and gates **Crash points**, **Intent rollback**, **In-process multi-runner**, **Outbox delivery**, and **Singleton uniqueness**.

Public multi-runner construction MUST use real peer transport and a unique, directly reachable advertised address per runner. `Runner.socket` MUST refuse PGlite and wildcard advertisement. The first public runner MUST record its shard count and expiration; mismatching runners and embedded joins without peer transport MUST fail before registering or taking shards. Layout changes MUST stop every runner first. Readiness MUST report `routing` until healthy registration and acquisition of every currently assigned shard, including the holder group; it does not promise activation or static assignment. Drain MUST make a runner unready before stopping claims, and layer closure MUST release shards. [ADR 0068](../decisions/0068-production-multi-runner.md) records configuration and evidence limits.
