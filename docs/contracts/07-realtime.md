# Realtime continuity

**Responsibility:** define subscriptions, replay, and connection behavior.  
**Authority:** normative.  
**Owner role:** realtime/runtime.  
**Change policy:** every transport must test snapshot/live race, loss, replay, and revocation.

Durable event subscriptions MUST use an event cursor and MUST preserve actor-stream order across replay and live delivery. Retention gaps MUST be explicit; clients MUST NOT be told that a feed is continuous when events can have been skipped. The same rules bind actor-to-actor subscriptions ([ADR 0026](../decisions/0026-cross-actor-event-subscriptions.md)): per-source cursor order, and a `RetentionGap` delivery to the subscriber's handler instead of a silent skip. An id-routed subscription has no recipient for a gap; it records and counts the gap instead.

`X.Read` and `X.Connection` MUST expose committed state only. Committed state changes MUST publish only after commit, and browser handles MUST receive them to reconcile optimistic reducers. Workflow bodies and effect executors do not receive direct actor-state access.

`Actor.stream` is live and non-persisted. Each subscription runs its handler on the activation, and an open subscription keeps the activation resident. It ends with the activation, and the subscriber MUST receive that end as `ActorError` reason `SessionEnded` with cause `ActivationEnded`, never as a silent completion. A stream does not promise replay; a stream handler that needs continuity uses `read.follow(Event, { after })`, which replays after the exclusive cursor and then follows commits with no gap or repeat. Durable output uses `events(Event, { after })`, whose cursor is exclusive, and yields the committed event with its cursor (its per-actor sequence), timestamp, and command id. A cursor the actor never issued MUST fail with `UnknownCursor`, and a cursor followed by pruned history MUST fail with `RetentionGap`; replay MUST NOT resume past either.

Realtime sessions MUST be declared with `Actor.connection`. A runtime's transport, not the activation, MUST hold each socket; the runner whose transport accepted it is the connection's holder. Connection session state MUST be stored in `actor_connections`, scoped to tenant and actor, and limited to 16 KiB of encoded state. With `policy.connections: "park"` (the default), open sockets MUST NOT keep the activation resident: hibernation leaves sockets open, and an open or an inbound frame wakes the activation, on whichever runner owns the actor. Connection messages MUST NOT pass through the command mailbox or count against `mailboxCapacity`. A broadcast MAY wake a parked actor through the trigger that requests it: every broadcast is requested by a committed turn (from a command, intent, timer, cron tick, effect route, or cross-actor subscription event) or by a connection handler, and those paths wake the actor. A woken activation MUST load its connection list before its first broadcast flushes, so the broadcast MUST reach every connection open when its turn committed, parked or not, on any runner. There is no out-of-turn broadcast; framework-originated frames such as executor progress are routed through the owner activation, which they wake. A handler running on an activation that did not run the connection's `open` MUST observe the restored session and `resumed === true` on `X.Connection`. `policy.connections: "keepAwake"` counts open connections as activity but does not guarantee residency.

Connection handlers MUST run with the actor's tenant and the caller stored at open, never a caller taken from frame content, and the build Effect MUST NOT run as a connection's caller. Sessions MUST NOT hold credentials. Inbound frames and params are limited to 64 KiB, connections to 10,000 per member per actor, and stream subscriptions to 256 per actor; holders bound total outbound buffering. Connection handlers MUST NOT write actor state, rows, events, or blobs; durable changes are commands, which get command ids derived from a per-connection secret and the frame sequence, stable across redelivery and unpredictable to other callers, so a redelivered frame does not execute them twice. Frames of one connection MUST be handled in order. A handler's change to its connection's session is written after the handler returns, fenced by the actor's generation and ordered by the frame's sequence number, so a redelivered frame changes the stored session at most once. An `open` MUST NOT miss a broadcast committed while it runs. See [ADR 0023](../decisions/0023-connections-parking-and-streams.md).

`broadcast` is best-effort and non-transactional. Durable delivery MUST use events or command intents instead. Verification is the **Connection park** gate plus realtime invariants in [invariants](../verification/invariants.md).

**Target exception: executor progress frames** ([ADR 0030](../decisions/0030-executor-progress-frames.md), proposed; binds once M2.18 implements it). An effect executor MAY report progress before its result commits. Progress frames are the one labelled exception to "only committed state publishes": they are pre-commit and best-effort, and they are never actor state, events, receipts, or outbox rows. They reach only the performing actor's own connections whose member opts in (by default only those whose caller is the effect's principal) and stream members that list the effect, always through the owner activation, in the actor's tenant, under each session's authorization. Unlike member frames, they MAY be coalesced or dropped, never cause `SlowConsumer`, carry no event cursor, and are never replayed; a loss followed by a later frame of the same attempt shows as a gap in its per-attempt `seq`, and a lost last frame shows nothing. No progress frame for an effect is delivered after that effect's route or cancellation commits; an effect with no route stops showing progress within 5 seconds of its settle.

Parking preserves a socket across activation hibernation and across the loss of the actor's owner, not across the death of the process holding that socket. When the owner dies ungracefully, its broadcasts may have been lost, so the holder MUST keep the socket open and send the connection a `Resync` control frame before any frame from the new owner. Frames are stamped with the activation's flushed-through event cursor, and `Resync.after` is the last such cursor the holder received from the dead owner (or the connection's open baseline, if later); it is exclusive, a lower bound, and covers only frames that mirror events. Clients MUST reload any state derived from frames that carry no event. An activation MUST announce itself to a holder before its first message of any kind to it, so an owner that dies after sending anything is still detected, and MUST collect seal acknowledgments before releasing its shard; a holder it cannot reach is excluded and closes its connections within `min(reauthorizeEvery, 10 seconds)`. Clients deduplicate on the event cursor a frame carries, never on the watermark. The client MUST treat earlier live state as possibly incomplete, resynchronize by replaying events after `after` or reloading state, and acknowledge with `ResyncDone`; a member's optional `resync` handler may replay for it, after which the holder sends `ResyncReplayed`. A connection that is not acknowledged within 30 seconds, or whose resync cannot run, MUST close with `SessionEnded` cause `OwnerLost` and `resync: true`. Frames from the dead owner that arrive after `Resync` MUST be dropped. Only the holder creates `Resync` and consumes `ResyncDone`; control frames MUST travel apart from member frames. `resync` handlers run at bounded concurrency, never without a baseline cursor, within the outbound limits, and a third `Resync` within 5 minutes closes the connection. Transport loss requires reconnect, as a new connection with a fresh session, and, for durable output, event replay. Authorization applies to replay and live delivery; slow consumers and retention gaps require explicit resync or disconnect behavior rather than silent loss. A connection whose outbound buffer would exceed 1,024 frames or 1 MiB, or whose holder detects a gap in the owner's broadcasts, MUST be closed with `SessionEnded` cause `SlowConsumer` and `resync: true`; a stream whose 256-element window stays full for 30 seconds MUST end the same way. Single frames and stream elements MUST NOT be dropped or coalesced.

Connections and subscriptions MUST reauthorize or disconnect within the revocation bound, `policy.reauthorizeEvery` (default 60 seconds, from 1 second to 1 hour). This bound covers sessions held for an external principal; actor-to-actor subscriptions are accepted internal work and are stopped by `turn.unsubscribe` instead ([ADR 0026](../decisions/0026-cross-actor-event-subscriptions.md)). The bound counts from the last successful check. The holder MUST reauthorize each connection, and the owner each stream subscription, on a timer, whether or not it is parked or idle; the holder MUST NOT forward a frame on authorization older than the bound; both MUST close the session at the bound even while a check is in flight, measured on a monotonic clock; the owner MUST NOT run a handler for a frame past the `authorizedUntil` it carries; and revocation MUST discard the session's buffered outbound and unacknowledged inbound frames without running `close`. Parking, resumption, reconnect, in-place resync, and event replay MUST NOT extend stale access beyond the bound; a resync request carries `authorizedUntil` like a frame, and revocation cancels a pending resync. Losing session access does not cancel previously accepted durable work; that requires explicit cancellation under the [security contract](10-security.md).

Ephemeral high-frequency data, such as cursors and typing indicators, MUST travel as connection frames rather than commands; there is no lower-durability command mode.
